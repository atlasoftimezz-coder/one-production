# Script Structures - Proven Formats

> **OUTPUT MODE NOTE:** Templates below show **Traditional Editing mode** with timestamps and `[VISUAL:]` cues.
> For **Vidrush mode**: Use same structures but (1) remove all timestamps, (2) remove all `[VISUAL:]` brackets, (3) embed visual keywords naturally in prose, (4) add Four Pillars header. See master file for Vidrush details.

## The 3-Act Framework (Foundation)

All structures follow this foundation:

### ACT 1: DEPARTURE (First 15-20%)
- Hook the viewer
- Establish context
- Open the main loop

### ACT 2: INITIATION (Middle 60-70%)
- Deliver main content
- Build through tension-resolution cycles
- Stack mini-payoffs with rehooks

### ACT 3: RETURN (Final 15-20%)
- Deliver grand payoff
- Connect all pieces
- Clear call to action

## STRUCTURE 1: THE LISTICLE

**Best for:** "X things you didn't know," "Top 10," rankings, collections

### Template (10-12 minutes / 1,500-1,800 words)

```
HOOK (0:00-0:45) [~100 words]
[VISUAL: Most dramatic item preview or result shot]

Why this list matters to YOU specifically.
What you'll be able to do after watching.
Stakes of not knowing these things.

---

CONTEXT (0:45-1:30) [~100 words]

Brief setup for the list.
Why these items were selected.
Open loop: "And #1 is something most people get completely wrong..."

---

ITEM [X] (1:30-3:00) [~200 words each]

[NUMBER ON SCREEN]

Setup: What this item is
Why it matters: The problem it solves or insight it provides
Proof: Specific example or data point
[VISUAL: Supporting footage or graphic]

Rehook to next item:
"But this next one is even more important because..."

[REPEAT for each item]

---

GRAND PAYOFF (Last 2 minutes) [~200 words]

How these items connect together.
The bigger pattern or insight.
What to do with this knowledge.

[VISUAL: All items summarized on screen]

---

CTA [~100 words]

Natural next step.
Related video recommendation.
```

### Pro Tips for Listicles:
- **Countdown (10 to 1):** Creates anticipation, saves best for last
- **Countup (1 to 10):** Builds energy, good for ranking worst to best
- **Best item in MIDDLE:** Keeps retention through the whole video
- **Each item must have a visual change** - never just talk over same footage

## STRUCTURE 2: THE DOCUMENTARY

**Best for:** Stories, histories, biographies, case studies, investigations

### Template (12-20 minutes / 1,800-3,000 words)

```
COLD OPEN (0:00-1:00) [~150 words]

[VISUAL: Dramatic moment from MIDDLE of story]

Most intense or surprising moment.
Maximum curiosity gap.

[TEXT ON SCREEN: "6 months earlier..." or "But to understand how we got here..."]

---

SETUP - THE ORDINARY WORLD (1:00-3:00) [~300 words]

Who is the main character?
What was their normal life like?
What world did they exist in?

[VISUAL: Establishing shots, context imagery]

Inciting incident: What changed everything?

---

RISING ACTION - THE JOURNEY (3:00-10:00) [~1,000 words]

PHASE 1: First major event
- Setup → Tension → Resolution
- Rehook: "But this was just the beginning..."

PHASE 2: Complications build
- New obstacles emerge
- Stakes increase
- Character makes choices

PHASE 3: The approach
- Everything building toward climax
- Highest tension
- Point of no return

[Each phase connected by BUT/THEREFORE, never AND THEN]

---

CLIMAX (10:00-14:00) [~400 words]

The moment everything changes.
Maximum stakes.
The decision or revelation.

[VISUAL: Most impactful imagery]

What happened? (Resolve the cold open)

---

RESOLUTION (14:00-18:00) [~400 words]

Aftermath: What happened next?
What was learned?
How did things end up?

Connection to viewer's life:
"And this matters to you because..."

---

CTA [~100 words]

Natural transition to next content.
```

### Documentary Techniques:
- **Cold open from middle:** Creates massive loop, makes them need to watch
- **But/Therefore connections:** Every beat connected by conflict, not sequence
- **Visual changes every 1.5 seconds:** Documentaries need constant visual movement
- **Re-establish stakes regularly:** Remind them why this matters

## STRUCTURE 3: THE VIDEO ESSAY

**Best for:** Arguments, analysis, opinion pieces, contrarian takes, frameworks

### Template (10-15 minutes / 1,500-2,200 words)

```
THESIS STATEMENT (0:00-1:00) [~150 words]

[VISUAL: Bold text of main claim]

Your contrarian position stated clearly.
Why the common belief is wrong.
What you're going to prove.

"Most people believe X. But I'm going to show you why Y is actually true."

---

CONTEXT - WHY THIS MATTERS (1:00-2:30) [~200 words]

Why should they care about this argument?
What's at stake if they believe the wrong thing?
Who is this for?

---

POINT 1 - THE EVIDENCE (2:30-5:00) [~350 words]

Your first major supporting argument.

Claim: State the point
Evidence: Specific proof (data, example, story)
Connection: How this proves your thesis

[VISUAL: Supporting graphics or footage]

Rehook: "But this is just the beginning. The real proof is in..."

---

POINT 2 - THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE (5:00-8:00) [~400 words]

Your most compelling argument.
This is the heart of your essay.

[Put best point in middle for retention]

Deeper analysis.
Counter-argument addressed.
Why this changes everything.

Rehook: "Now you might be thinking [objection]. Here's why that's wrong..."

---

POINT 3 - THE IMPLICATIONS (8:00-10:30) [~300 words]

What does this mean for the viewer?
How should they think differently?
What should they do differently?

---

SYNTHESIS (10:30-12:00) [~200 words]

[VISUAL: All points connected]

How all three points connect.
The bigger picture revealed.
Your thesis restated with now-obvious truth.

---

CTA (12:00-13:00) [~150 words]

What to do with this new understanding.
Next content for deeper exploration.
```

### Video Essay Techniques:
- **State thesis immediately:** Don't bury the lead
- **Address counter-arguments:** Shows intellectual honesty
- **Use "you" throughout:** It's about THEIR thinking, not yours
- **End with transformation:** They should feel different than when they started

## STRUCTURE 4: THE EXPLAINER

**Best for:** How-to, tutorials, processes, frameworks, systems

### Template (8-12 minutes / 1,200-1,800 words)

```
HOOK - THE PROBLEM (0:00-0:45) [~100 words]

[VISUAL: Problem visualization]

The specific problem they have.
Why common solutions fail.
What you're going to show them.

---

CREDIBILITY BRIDGE (0:45-1:30) [~100 words]

Why should they trust YOU on this?
Brief proof of expertise.
Results you've achieved with this method.

---

OVERVIEW (1:30-2:00) [~75 words]

[VISUAL: Framework overview graphic]

Quick preview of the system/framework.
"There are 4 parts to this..."
Sets expectations for what's coming.

---

STEP 1 (2:00-4:00) [~250 words]

[TEXT ON SCREEN: "STEP 1: [Name]"]

What: Explain the step
Why: Why it matters
How: Specific instructions
Example: Show it in action

[VISUAL: Demonstration or example]

Transition: "Once you've done this, you're ready for..."

---

STEP 2 (4:00-6:00) [~250 words]
[Same structure as Step 1]

---

STEP 3 (6:00-8:00) [~250 words]
[Same structure as Step 1]

---

STEP 4 (8:00-9:30) [~200 words]
[Same structure as Step 1]

---

INTEGRATION (9:30-10:30) [~150 words]

[VISUAL: All steps shown together]

How all steps work together.
Common mistakes to avoid.
Expected results when done correctly.

---

CTA (10:30-11:00) [~100 words]

Clear next action.
Resources mentioned.
Related content.
```

### Explainer Techniques:
- **Show before explain:** Demonstrate, then break down
- **30/60 rule:** 30 seconds theory, 60 seconds application
- **Number your steps:** Gives progress indicators
- **End each step with transition:** Never leave dead air between sections

## STRUCTURE 5: THE COMPARISON

**Best for:** Versus videos, reviews, decision guides, rankings

### Template (8-10 minutes / 1,200-1,500 words)

```
HOOK - THE STAKES (0:00-0:45) [~100 words]

[VISUAL: Both options shown]

Why this decision matters.
What's at stake with the wrong choice.
Promise of clarity by end of video.

---

QUICK OVERVIEW (0:45-1:30) [~100 words]

[VISUAL: Side-by-side comparison graphic]

Brief intro to both options.
What we'll be comparing.
How you'll be able to decide by the end.

---

OPTION A DEEP DIVE (1:30-4:00) [~350 words]

[VISUAL: Option A focus]

What it is
Pros (be specific)
Cons (be honest)
Best use case

"Now let's see how Option B stacks up..."

---

OPTION B DEEP DIVE (4:00-6:30) [~350 words]

[VISUAL: Option B focus]

What it is
Pros (be specific)
Cons (be honest)
Best use case

"Now here's where it gets interesting..."

---

HEAD-TO-HEAD (6:30-8:00) [~200 words]

[VISUAL: Direct comparison graphics]

Category 1: Winner is...
Category 2: Winner is...
Category 3: Winner is...

---

THE VERDICT (8:00-9:00) [~150 words]

[VISUAL: Final recommendation graphic]

Who should choose Option A.
Who should choose Option B.
Your personal pick and why.

---

CTA (9:00-9:30) [~75 words]

Next comparison video.
Resources for chosen option.
```

### Comparison Techniques:
- **Be genuinely fair:** Viewers can smell bias
- **Give clear verdict:** Don't be wishy-washy
- **Segment by use case:** "For beginners, choose X. For pros, choose Y."
- **Visual side-by-sides:** Show, don't just tell

## STRUCTURE 6: THE FRAMEWORK REVEAL

**Best for:** Sharing your unique methodology, intellectual property, systems

### Template (12-15 minutes / 1,800-2,200 words)

```
HOOK - THE RESULT (0:00-1:00) [~150 words]

[VISUAL: Proof of results]

The specific result this framework produced.
For whom and in what timeframe.
Why existing approaches don't work.

---

ORIGIN STORY (1:00-2:30) [~200 words]

How you discovered/developed this.
What problem you were trying to solve.
The insight that changed everything.

---

FRAMEWORK OVERVIEW (2:30-3:30) [~150 words]

[VISUAL: Framework diagram]

Name of the framework.
High-level components.
Promise of what each part delivers.

---

COMPONENT 1 (3:30-5:30) [~300 words]

[VISUAL: Component 1 highlighted in framework]

What it is and why it matters.
Common mistake in this area.
How to do it right.
Real example.

Transition: "But this only works if you have Component 2..."

---

COMPONENT 2 (5:30-7:30) [~300 words]
[Same structure]

---

COMPONENT 3 (7:30-9:30) [~300 words]
[Same structure]

---

INTEGRATION (9:30-11:00) [~200 words]

[VISUAL: Complete framework]

How components work together.
Why the order matters.
What happens when all pieces are in place.

---

CASE STUDY (11:00-13:00) [~250 words]

[VISUAL: Specific results]

Real example of framework in action.
Specific numbers and outcomes.
What they did and what happened.

---

CTA (13:00-14:00) [~150 words]

How to implement this.
Next steps for going deeper.
Related content or offers.
```

### Framework Reveal Techniques:
- **Name your framework:** Makes it memorable and yours
- **Show the visual:** People remember diagrams
- **Use real proof:** Case studies with specific numbers
- **Build dependency:** Each component needs the others

## TIMING BENCHMARKS (All Formats)

- **Hook complete:** By 0:45-1:00
- **First mini-payoff:** By 2:00-2:30
- **Best content in middle:** 40-60% mark
- **Grand payoff foreshadowed:** At 30%, 60%, 90%
- **CTA:** Final 30-60 seconds

## STRUCTURE 7: THE CONCEPTUAL EXPLAINER

**Best for:** "How X works," "Why X happens," educational breakdowns, concept explanations, science/tech deep dives

> **Note:** This is different from Structure 4 (The Explainer), which is step-by-step "how-to" format. This format is for explaining CONCEPTS and IDEAS through progressive layers of understanding.

### The Progressive Complexity Framework

Explainers fail when they dump complexity on viewers too early. Build understanding in layers.

### Template (10-15 minutes / 1,500-2,200 words)

```
HOOK (0:00-0:45) [~100 words]
[VISUAL: Dramatic example of the concept in action]

Start with the RESULT or CONSEQUENCE of the concept.
"Every time you [common action], [surprising thing] happens. And almost nobody understands why."

Open loop: What's really going on behind the scenes?

---

LAYER 1: THE SIMPLE VERSION (0:45-3:00) [~300 words]

Explain the concept as if talking to a smart 12-year-old.
No jargon. One core idea. One clear analogy.

"Think of it like [everyday analogy]..."

[VISUAL: Simple animation/graphic showing the basic concept]

---

LAYER 2: THE REAL VERSION (3:00-6:00) [~400 words]

Now add nuance. Introduce the complexity that makes this interesting.
"But here's where it gets interesting..."

Add specific examples, data points, expert perspectives.
Each new detail builds on Layer 1. Never contradicts it.

[VISUAL: More detailed graphic or real-world footage]

---

LAYER 3: THE IMPLICATIONS (6:00-9:00) [~400 words]

So what? Why does this matter to the viewer's life?
Connect the concept to real-world consequences.
"This is why [thing they care about] works the way it does."

---

LAYER 4: THE TWIST / DEEPER TRUTH (9:00-12:00) [~400 words]

The non-obvious insight. The thing that changes how they see it.
"But what nobody talks about is..."

This is your unique angle. The reason this video exists instead of the 50 other explainers on this topic.

---

OUTRO (12:00-13:00) [~200 words]

Tie back to the hook. The viewer now understands what they didn't before.
Single CTA.
```

### Explainer Hook Patterns

1. **The Misconception Hook:** "Everything you've been told about [X] is wrong. Here's what actually happens."
2. **The Consequence Hook:** "Every time you [common action], [surprising consequence]. And you've probably never thought about why."
3. **The Scale Hook:** "[Massive number] people do [X] every day. Almost none of them know [surprising fact]."
4. **The "How Does This Work" Hook:** "You've used [X] a thousand times. But do you actually know how it works?"

### Key Differences From Documentary

| Explainer | Documentary |
|-----------|-------------|
| Concept-driven (ideas) | Character/event-driven (stories) |
| Progressive complexity (build understanding) | Chronological or thematic (build narrative) |
| Viewer learns something | Viewer experiences something |
| Visuals illustrate concepts | Visuals show real footage |
| CTA: "Now you understand" | CTA: "The story continues" |
