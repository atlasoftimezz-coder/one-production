# The Hero's Journey for YouTube Scriptwriters

> **OUTPUT MODE NOTE:** Visual cues like `[B-ROLL:]` shown here are for **Traditional mode**. For **Vidrush mode**, use the same narrative structure but embed visual keywords naturally in prose instead. See master file for details.

This framework isn't fluff. It's the invisible skeleton of every video that holds attention, drives retention, and emotionally hooks the viewer.

Use this like a story engineer... not your typical "content creator."

---

## ACT ONE: DEPARTURE

### 1. The Ordinary World
**Purpose:** Set the emotional baseline.

Show your subject's "normal." This is what gives your story contrast later.

**What to include:**
- Where they came from
- Their circumstances before everything changed
- The world they inhabited
- Their fears, hopes, or defining traits

**Example (Pablo Escobar):**
*Escobar as a quiet boy who admired his mother's strength and feared poverty.*

**Script notation:**
```
[B-ROLL: Childhood photos or location footage]
Before [SUBJECT] became [what they're known for], they were [ordinary state].
[Specific detail about their normal life].
```

---

### 2. Call to Adventure
**Purpose:** Something happens that triggers action.

Introduce an opportunity, challenge, or shift in perspective.

**What to include:**
- The inciting incident
- What changed in their world
- The choice they faced

**Example (Pablo Escobar):**
*Escobar realized the only path to power in Medellín was through violence or corruption.*

**Script notation:**
```
But then, [inciting incident happened].
[SUBJECT] realized [the realization that changed everything].
```

---

### 3. Refusal of the Call
**Purpose:** The subject hesitates.

Show moral conflict, fear, or initial pushback. **This makes the payoff stronger later.**

**What to include:**
- Their hesitation or resistance
- The internal conflict
- Why this wasn't an easy choice

**Example (Pablo Escobar):**
*His early struggle to choose between education or street life.*

**Script notation:**
```
At first, [SUBJECT] resisted.
[Show the conflict or hesitation].
But [what pushed them forward anyway].
```

**Why this matters:** Without resistance, there's no tension. The viewer needs to feel that this decision had weight.

---

### 4. Meeting the Mentor / Crossing the Threshold
**Purpose:** Someone or something pushes them forward.

It could be a friend, trauma, discovery... or simply an inner decision.

**What to include:**
- The person, event, or realization that pushed them
- The point of no return
- Their first step into the "new world"

**Example (Pablo Escobar):**
*He meets future cartel allies and takes his first "illegal" job.*

**Script notation:**
```
That's when [SUBJECT] met [mentor/catalyst].
[What happened that pushed them across the threshold].
There was no going back.
```

---

## ACT TWO: INITIATION

### 5. Tests, Allies, Enemies
**Purpose:** The rise begins.

Introduce challenges, alliances, betrayals. **Build rhythm and emotional stakes.**

**What to include:**
- Key allies they gained
- Enemies they made
- Obstacles they overcame
- Early victories and setbacks

**Example (Pablo Escobar):**
*Escobar gaining trust from key players, while dodging law enforcement.*

**Script notation:**
```
[SUBJECT] quickly learned who to trust, and who wanted them dead.

[Ally 1] became [their role].
But [Enemy 1] had other plans.

[Specific challenge they faced and overcame].
```

**Pacing note:** This section can be the longest. Use multiple mini-stories with BUT/THEREFORE connections.

---

### 6. Approach to the Inmost Cave
**Purpose:** Things escalate. **This is the "uh-oh" moment.**

Introduce a mystery, moral dilemma, or major turning point.

**What to include:**
- The moment things got serious
- A line being crossed
- The point where stakes became life-or-death

**Example (Pablo Escobar):**
*He assassinates a rival and takes control, crossing a line he can't come back from.*

**Script notation:**
```
But [SUBJECT] was about to cross a line they could never come back from.

[The specific moment/decision].

[TEXT ON SCREEN: Key date or event name]

Everything was about to change.
```

---

### 7. The Supreme Ordeal
**Purpose:** The biggest moment of tension.

This is where the viewer thinks, **"Can they actually survive this?"**

**What to include:**
- The crisis point
- Maximum danger or conflict
- Everything falling apart

**Example (Pablo Escobar):**
*The bombing, the fallout, the paranoia. Everything falls apart.*

**Script notation:**
```
[MUSIC: Build tension]

Then came [the supreme ordeal].

[Vivid description of the crisis].

[SUBJECT] had never faced anything like this.

[Show the desperation, the stakes, the near-destruction].
```

**Retention note:** This should be your most gripping section. Slow down. Let the tension breathe.

---

### 8. Seizing the Sword
**Purpose:** The subject gains insight, power, or clarity.

But it's not a happy ending. **It's growth through pain.**

**What to include:**
- What they gained from the ordeal
- The cost of that gain
- Their transformation (for better or worse)

**Example (Pablo Escobar):**
*Escobar tries to negotiate with the government while losing everything around him.*

**Script notation:**
```
Through it all, [SUBJECT] gained [what they learned/achieved].

But the cost was [what they lost].

They were no longer the same person who [reference to ordinary world].
```

---

## ACT THREE: RETURN

### 9. The Road Back
**Purpose:** We return to the emotional core.

Strip away the noise. Focus on consequences and the shift in perspective.

**What to include:**
- The beginning of the end
- Consequences catching up
- Return to what matters

**Example (Pablo Escobar):**
*His downfall begins. He's isolated, hunted, and haunted by his own empire.*

**Script notation:**
```
But empires don't last forever.

[The beginning of the downfall].

[SUBJECT] was now [their diminished state].

The same [thing that gave them power] was now [destroying them].
```

---

### 10. Resurrection
**Purpose:** Your final peak.

Not a twist, but an emotional crescendo. Make the audience feel what all of this meant.

**What to include:**
- The climactic moment
- The final confrontation or resolution
- The emotional weight of everything that came before

**Example (Pablo Escobar):**
*His death isn't just an end... it's a symbol of legacy, loss, and the price of obsession.*

**Script notation:**
```
[MUSIC: Emotional peak]

On [date], [the final event].

[Describe it with weight and meaning].

[Let this moment breathe. Don't rush.]
```

---

### 11. Return with the Elixir
**Purpose:** Bring it full circle.

End with a reflective message, insight, or irony. **Stick the damn landing.**

**What to include:**
- The lesson or meaning
- Connection back to the beginning
- Final reflection or irony

**Example (Pablo Escobar):**
*The same street he conquered becomes his grave. The empire crumbles. The world moves on.*

**Script notation:**
```
[B-ROLL: Return to opening imagery or location]

[Reflective statement connecting end to beginning].

[Final insight about what this story means].

[Optional: What happened to other key figures]
```

## APPLYING THE FRAMEWORK

### For Biographical Documentaries (15-25 min)

| Act | Timing | Content |
|-----|--------|---------|
| Act 1: Departure | 0:00-5:00 | Ordinary World → Call → Refusal → Threshold |
| Act 2: Initiation | 5:00-18:00 | Tests → Approach → Ordeal → Sword |
| Act 3: Return | 18:00-23:00 | Road Back → Resurrection → Elixir |

### For Shorter Docs (10-15 min)

Compress by combining:
- Steps 1-4 into one "Origin" section (2-3 min)
- Steps 5-8 into one "Rise & Fall" section (5-8 min)
- Steps 9-11 into one "Aftermath" section (2-3 min)

### For True Crime

Same structure, but:
- "Supreme Ordeal" = The crime itself or the investigation's darkest moment
- "Resurrection" = Capture, trial, or revelation
- "Elixir" = Justice served (or not), lessons learned

---

## THE BUT/THEREFORE TEST

Every transition between steps should be connected by BUT or THEREFORE, not AND THEN.

**Bad:**
"He became powerful AND THEN he got enemies AND THEN things went wrong."

**Good:**
"He became powerful, BUT that power attracted enemies, THEREFORE he had to make brutal choices, BUT those choices would eventually destroy him."
